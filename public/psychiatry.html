What is psychiatry?

When your mind needs medical support

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who specialises in mental health. Psychiatric care looks at the biological, psychological and social factors behind what you are experiencing.

A consultation may help if symptoms are severe, have lasted a long time, or are not improving with therapy alone. Your psychiatrist may recommend medication, therapy, lifestyle changes or a combination — and will always explain options clearly so you can make informed choices.

  • Comprehensive initial assessment (60–90 minutes)
  • Shorter follow-up reviews (20–30 minutes)
  • Coordinated care with our therapists and nutritionists
  • In person in Nairobi or via secure telepsychiatry

Therapist or psychiatrist?

Understanding the difference

Psychotherapist / Counsellor

  • Talk-based support and practical tools
  • Explores thoughts, emotions, relationships and patterns
  • Weekly or fortnightly sessions
  • Does not prescribe medication

Psychiatrist

  • Medical doctor specialising in mental health
  • Assesses, diagnoses and treats conditions
  • Can prescribe and manage medication
  • Often works alongside a therapist

FAQs

Questions about psychiatry

Do I need a referral?
No referral is needed. You can book directly. If you have previous reports or prescriptions, please bring or share them.
Will I have to take medication?
Not necessarily. Medication is one option among several. Your psychiatrist will discuss benefits, risks and alternatives, and the decision is made together with you.
Can prescriptions be issued online?
Where clinically appropriate and permitted by local regulations, prescriptions can be issued after a telepsychiatry consultation. Some situations may require an in-person review.
What should I prepare for my first appointment?
A list of current medications, any previous diagnoses or reports, and notes about your main concerns. Bringing a trusted person is welcome if it helps you.
How often are follow-ups?
Usually every 2–6 weeks when starting or changing medication, then less often once you are stable.
